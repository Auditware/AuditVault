# Mercado Bitcoin -  Rekt

> **Loss:** $48,600,000 | **Date:** 2026-11-02 | **Audited by:** N/A

![](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/RektHQ/Assets/main/images/2023/01/mercado-rekt-header.png)





_Six days before the hack, [Mercado Bitcoin applied to Brazil's central bank for authorization to operate as a regulated crypto brokerage, custody included](https://www.letsmoney.com.br/cripto/mercado-bitcoin-pede-licenca-bc-corretora-cripto/)._

**Its CEO [said the exchange had invested early in security](https://www.letsmoney.com.br/cripto/mercado-bitcoin-pede-licenca-bc-corretora-cripto/).**

On November 2, [an attacker drained its hot wallets on 11 chains](https://bitquery.io/investigations/mercado-bitcoin-hack), then went to work on the customer deposit addresses.

At 12:56 UTC, [Brazil's largest crypto exchange](https://therecord.media/mercado-bitcoin-brazil-cryptocurrency-heist) announced it had [identified and quickly contained the incident, with no harm to customers](https://x.com/MercadoBitcoin/status/2117239507263132056).

[The Ethereum deposit sweep was already half an hour old](https://bitquery.io/investigations/mercado-bitcoin-hack), and it ran for four more.

By 16:56 UTC, [36,042 Ethereum deposit addresses](https://bitquery.io/investigations/mercado-bitcoin-hack) had each signed their balances over to the attacker, and Bitquery counts [$48.6 million gone across 11 blockchains](https://bitquery.io/investigations/mercado-bitcoin-hack).

**[Bitquery also traces funds from wallets tied to BitMart into the same attacker address](https://bitquery.io/investigations/mercado-bitcoin-hack), starting 53 minutes before the Mercado Bitcoin drain.**

_If the incident was contained, who was still signing?_

**Tether moved before Mercado Bitcoin said a word.**

At 10:21 UTC on November 2, [the stablecoin issuer froze the attacker's USDT0 on Polygon](https://bitquery.io/investigations/mercado-bitcoin-hack), then its USDT on Tron and Ethereum about an hour later.

[Mercado Bitcoin's first public statement](https://x.com/MercadoBitcoin/status/2117239507263132056) was still more than two and a half hours away.

The theft itself was older than that. [Between 06:24 and 06:48 UTC](https://bitquery.io/investigations/mercado-bitcoin-hack), the exchange's hot wallets on ten chains paid the same attacker.

_On Ethereum, [188 transfers moved $29.5 million in 24 minutes](https://bitquery.io/investigations/mercado-bitcoin-hack). The hot wallet sent 2,852 ETH first, then USDT and USDC, then token after token, down to scraps worth a few dollars._

**[Hypernative flagged more than $40 million in suspicious transfers](https://x.com/PabloSabbatella/status/2117241590787846164) across at least ten chains.**  
  
[In an alert shared by Opsek founder Pablo Sabbatella](https://x.com/PabloSabbatella/status/2117241590787846164), it described plain transfers, not a smart contract exploit, and said it was [](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/crypto/articles/mercado-bitcoin-halts-withdrawals-over-153238091.html) how the attacker obtained signing access is not yet known.

No elaborate onchain exploit was visible. Just an exchange's own wallets, paying out.

On Ethereum, [the stolen funds then sat untouched for roughly five hours](https://bitquery.io/investigations/mercado-bitcoin-hack).

_Just after midday UTC, [2,800 ETH moved to a fresh wallet](https://bitquery.io/investigations/mercado-bitcoin-hack). At 12:27, Mercado Bitcoin's customer deposit addresses began sending their balances to the attacker, one address at a time._

**Twenty-nine minutes later, the exchange spoke.**

["We identified and swiftly contained a security incident in one of our systems early this morning,"](https://x.com/MercadoBitcoin/status/2117239507263132056) Mercado Bitcoin posted at 12:56 UTC, adding that there had been no harm to customers and no data breach. Deposits and withdrawals were suspended as a precaution.

At 14:24 UTC, [the exchange published five attacker destination addresses on Twitter, covering EVM chains](https://x.com/MercadoBitcoin/status/2117261565200146525), Bitcoin, Solana, Stellar and Tron, "exclusively for identification and tracking purposes."

The tracking was already underway. "Tether froze more than 4.5M USDT related to this security incident," [Paolo Ardoino posted](https://x.com/paoloardoino/status/2117272224814219720).

**[Arkham analyst Emmett Gallic put the tally at](https://x.com/emmettgallic/status/2117285473676574896) “$46M stolen and growing.”**  
  
_**[In a later post, he wrote](https://x.com/emmettgallic/status/2117359161726075034):** “The Mercado Bitcoin Hacker is still sweeping thousands old deposit addresses.” “Not leaving a dollar behind.”_

At 16:56 UTC, the Ethereum deposit sweep finally stopped. [Bitquery counted 36,042 addresses,](https://bitquery.io/investigations/mercado-bitcoin-hack) roughly four hours after [Mercado’s "swiftly contained" announcement](https://x.com/MercadoBitcoin/status/2117239507263132056).

By evening, Mercado Bitcoin's language had shifted.

_The reassurance shifted from what had happened to what customers could expect: “No harm to customers” was followed by a promise that they [“will not suffer any loss of assets.”](https://x.com/MercadoBitcoin/status/2117395627529089233) The first asserted no customer harm; the second promised no customer loss._

**On Twitter, [the exchange put the assets involved at approximately $38 million](https://x.com/MercadoBitcoin/status/2117395630150795543), with $11 million blocked alongside Tether, Circle, Paxos, BRL1 and Chainalysis.**

That same evening in Brazíl, [Times Brasil reported the company's figure as roughly $49 million involved, with $11.2 million blocked](https://timesbrasil.com.br/cripto-brasil/mercado-bitcoin-diz-ter-bloqueado-us-112-milhoes-apos-invasao/).  
  
[Chainalysis's initial account also estimated about $49 million](https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/chainalysis-response-mercado-bitcoin/), describing attacker activity across 17 chains.

Two numbers, one exchange, one night. That gap would come back.

**Hot wallets are built to send money. But emptying thousands of deposit addresses required something broader: their keys, or access to whatever signed for them.**

_So who was telling 36,042 of them to pay a stranger?_

  
### Two Kinds of Yes

_**[Bitquery lays out the custody model](https://bitquery.io/investigations/mercado-bitcoin-hack):** Customers receive their own deposit addresses, deposits are credited to their accounts, and the coins are later swept into hot wallets that pay out withdrawals._

**The customer never holds those keys. The exchange does.**

That gives an exchange two different things to protect: The system that decides what its hot wallets pay, and the key material behind every deposit address it has ever issued.

On November 2, the attacker could obtain payments from both kinds of wallets.

Bitquery spotted the seam. [The large Bitcoin payment from Mercado Bitcoin's hot wallet looked like one of the exchange's ordinary payouts](https://bitquery.io/investigations/mercado-bitcoin-hack).  
  
_**[The Bitcoin deposit sweeps did not](https://bitquery.io/investigations/mercado-bitcoin-hack):** Different software, and far higher fees._

**One pattern looks like the exchange's own machinery doing the attacker's work. The other looks like someone outside that machinery, signing on their own.**

[Bitquery says the pattern fits access to the exchange's payout system plus access to the deposit keys](https://bitquery.io/investigations/mercado-bitcoin-hack), while cautioning that the chain alone cannot prove it.

The deposit sweep shows what that breadth of signing access was worth.

[Each of the 36,042 Ethereum addresses signed its own transfer of its full balance](https://bitquery.io/investigations/mercado-bitcoin-hack). Late in the run, the attacker used deposit addresses still holding ETH to pay network fees for others, so their tokens could be emptied too.

_It was patient work for small money. [Bitquery found that about 91% of the swept addresses held less than $10](https://bitquery.io/investigations/mercado-bitcoin-hack). The 113 holding $100 or more accounted for 61% of the $266,000 total._

**[The Ethereum hot wallet had given up $29.5 million in 24 minutes](https://bitquery.io/investigations/mercado-bitcoin-hack). The deposit addresses yielded less than 1% of that over four and a half hours.**

The sweep didn't stop at Ethereum. [Fifty-three Bitcoin deposit addresses were emptied](https://bitquery.io/investigations/mercado-bitcoin-hack). On BNB Chain, 143 addresses paid the attacker from 14:26 UTC, and a busy wallet Bitquery's labels tie to the exchange, one that had previously collected from 1,483 deposit addresses, sent $124,000 by 18:08 UTC.

On Base, [308 more deposit addresses paid a new attacker wallet in three minutes that evening](https://bitquery.io/investigations/mercado-bitcoin-hack), nearly five hours after "[swiftly contained.](https://x.com/MercadoBitcoin/status/2117239507263132056)"

Access to the underlying keys could explain how the attacker drained so many addresses. [Bitquery notes that exchanges commonly derive deposit keys from a master key](https://bitquery.io/investigations/mercado-bitcoin-hack). If the attacker obtained that root, the exposure could extend to other addresses derived from it, not just the ones already drained.

_That is also why the timeline matters. Disabling a payout system can stop requests that run through it. It does nothing to a private key someone else already holds._

**The groundwork was laid the night before. [The attacker's Ethereum address received its first coins on the evening of November 1](https://bitquery.io/investigations/mercado-bitcoin-hack), and its Tron address minutes later. Two small wallets that tested the Ethereum address also paid the Tron one, suggesting a single operator set up both.**

What the chain cannot show is the way in.

[The transactions establish valid signatures across hot wallets and thousands of deposit addresses](https://bitquery.io/investigations/mercado-bitcoin-hack). They don't say whether keys were extracted, a signing service was abused, a wallet-management system was hijacked, or an insider or vendor opened the door.

[Mercado Bitcoin has described the incident as occurring in "one of our systems."](https://x.com/MercadoBitcoin/status/2117239507263132056) In its public statements reviewed through November 9, Mercado Bitcoin did not identify the compromised system, explain how the attacker gained access, or say whether affected deposit addresses would be replaced.

[Bitget said its private keys were not compromised](https://www.bitget.com/academy/bitget-security-incident-what-happened-timeline-impact-response). Binance said it [found no evidence of a private-key leak](https://www.binance.com/en/support/announcement/detail/c5a8729a97ac486d963784d96c5642d7). In those same statements, Mercado Bitcoin offered no comparable assurance about its keys.  
  
**[Bitquery advises users not to send coins to an old Mercado Bitcoin deposit address until the exchange says it is safe](https://bitquery.io/investigations/mercado-bitcoin-hack), and to expect new deposit addresses.**

_With signing power that reached from hot wallets to pocket-change deposit addresses, where did the attacker take $48.6 million next?_

### Clearance Sale

_The $48.6 million headline is a valuation, not a cash balance._

**[Bitquery values each token at its price when it left the exchange and leaves out tokens](https://bitquery.io/investigations/mercado-bitcoin-hack) with no market price at all.**  
  
**[One holding makes the valuation problem especially clear](https://bitquery.io/investigations/mercado-bitcoin-hack):** EVA on Arbitrum counts for $1.21 million at its quoted price, but trades less than that in a typical week. Strip it out and the figure falls to $47.4 million. That quoted value does not mean the attacker could find enough buyers to sell the entire holding at that price.  
  
[Ethereum carried 61% of the loss, at $29.72 million](https://bitquery.io/investigations/mercado-bitcoin-hack). Bitcoin followed at $8.30 million, then Solana at $3.22 million, Stellar at $2.12 million, Arbitrum at $1.53 million and Tron at $1.51 million. Polygon, Avalanche, BNB Chain, XDC and Base made up the rest.

[Bitquery had observed no deposits of the stolen funds into a crypto exchange](https://bitquery.io/investigations/mercado-bitcoin-hack) within its tracing window.

Funds continued [to move from the attacker’s main Ethereum address on November 9](https://etherscan.io/address/0xe485e7A70b1131216Cb47CA4555c4Bbb2C742478#tokentxns).  
  
The inventory was still moving.

**The money left a trail across 11 chains. The attacker's address left one too, and part of it started somewhere other than Mercado Bitcoin.**

_Why had the same address received funds from wallets tied to BitMart 53 minutes before the main event?_

### View Only

_Mercado Bitcoin was not the attacker's first stop that night._

**[Bitquery traces about $298,000 into the same attacker address from wallets on Base, Arbitrum and Optimism](https://bitquery.io/investigations/mercado-bitcoin-hack), starting 53 minutes before the Mercado Bitcoin drain. Nearly all of it came from [three wallets Bitquery ties to BitMart](https://bitquery.io/investigations/mercado-bitcoin-hack).**

Bitquery's [labels mark the Arbitrum wallet as a BitMart hot wallet](https://bitquery.io/investigations/mercado-bitcoin-hack).  
  
**BitMart-Labeled Arbitrum Hot Wallet:**
[0xb4af4d0b9bd38c9f2fec9778e48667912f778f01](https://arbiscan.io/address/0xb4af4d0b9bd38c9f2fec9778e48667912f778f01)

The Base wallet is murkier. [Arkham's write-up dates the start of the attack to a 0.0001 ETH test from what it calls Mercado Bitcoin's Base hot wallet at 01:22 UTC](https://info.arkm.com/research/mercado-bitcoin-hack-trace-the-46m-stolen-on-chain).  
  
_**Bitquery, [which says one outside label set calls the wallet Mercado Bitcoin's, doubts it](https://bitquery.io/investigations/mercado-bitcoin-hack):** None of the wallet's past payers appear as Mercado Bitcoin deposit addresses in its data, and in 2024 the wallet traded small test payments with an address BitMart lists in its proof of reserves._

**A quick check of Arkham confirms it labels the wallet as Mercado Bitcoin’s.**  
  
**Base wallet labeled by Arkham as a Mercado Bitcoin hot wallet:**  
[0xc3F6b6c4309Ba9A9887216DbeBf39FAC78406696](https://basescan.org/address/0xc3F6b6c4309Ba9A9887216DbeBf39FAC78406696)  
  
**[The attacker came back to Base that evening](https://bitquery.io/investigations/mercado-bitcoin-hack):** At 20:20 UTC, 80 different tokens left that wallet in six minutes. Separately, between 20:19 and 20:44 UTC, about 1,400 Base deposit addresses each sent the attacker a few dollars in ETH.  
  
**[On Arbitrum](https://bitquery.io/investigations/mercado-bitcoin-hack):** 1,320 deposit addresses, three in four labeled BitMart’s by Bitquery, paid the wallet behind that Base sweep.  
  
_[Five smaller chains added about $30,000](https://bitquery.io/investigations/mercado-bitcoin-hack), valued at November 3 prices._  
  
**BitMart's founder Sheldon, confirmed the breach without confirming the number. On November 3, [Sheldon posted that BitMart had been hacked the day before](https://x.com/sheldonbitmart/status/2117650361154691529), called the amount small, and said a preliminary investigation pointed to the group behind the Mercado Bitcoin attack.**

[Sheldon also described a security team left severely understaffed amid restructuring](https://x.com/sheldonbitmart/status/2117650361154691529). He cited threats against employees and alleged retaliation by former staff, saying these had complicated efforts to retain technical personnel.

[BitMart announced a wind-down in July](https://bitmart.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/53544595916059-Important-Notice-Regarding-the-Orderly-Cessation-of-BitMart-Operations), scheduling the end of trading for August 26. [It lost $196 million in December 2021](https://rekt.news/bitmart-rekt), a theft its [CEO at the time attributed to a stolen private key](https://www.coindesk.com/tech/2021/12/06/bitmart-ceo-says-stolen-private-key-behind-196m-hack).

If the wallets are BitMart's, Bitquery writes, [the attacker held signing power at two exchanges on the same night](https://bitquery.io/investigations/mercado-bitcoin-hack).  
  
_[A shared supplier](https://bitquery.io/investigations/mercado-bitcoin-hack), such as a firm running wallets for both, would be worth examining._

**No shared vendor or common entry point has been established. In the statements reviewed for this story, neither exchange has said whether it uses one.**

Mercado Bitcoin, meanwhile, was busy reassuring.

On November 3, [the exchange told customers their assets were secured and their balances remained recorded in their accounts](https://x.com/MercadoBitcoin/status/2117637227551273267). It put the affected assets at $36.3 million, with $12.9 million blocked, and warned that it never asks for passwords, codes or transfers.

On November 9, the exchange [announced the gradual return of website access to check balances and holdings](https://x.com/MercadoBitcoin/status/2119766674723188751), with [displayed crypto balances reflecting customers’ pre-incident November 2 positions](https://x.com/MercadoBitcoin/status/2119766681874563196).  
  
_[Trading, withdrawals, deposits, transfers](https://x.com/MercadoBitcoin/status/2119766677193674799), Pix and card services remained unavailable._

**[The next phase, scheduled for the following week, includes account revalidation and Pix withdrawals restricted to accounts in customers’ own names](https://x.com/MercadoBitcoin/status/2119766689004847262). Trading and crypto transfers would return in later phases.**

[The exchange had promised to replace affected customer assets with its own resources](https://x.com/MercadoBitcoin/status/2118096168265892094). By November 9, [it said it was already replenishing those assets using its own funds](https://x.com/MercadoBitcoin/status/2119766691185861022).

One line of the exchange’s reassurance named a familiar partner.

[Tether invested $20 million in Mercado Bitcoin in July](https://cointelegraph.com/news/tether-invests-20m-in-mercado-bitcoin-to-expand-tokenized-finance-in-latin-america) to support its expansion into tokenized assets and other blockchain-based financial services

_That investor also became the first issuer to freeze funds linked to the attack. [Bitquery puts Tether’s frozen total at $5.9 million](https://bitquery.io/investigations/mercado-bitcoin-hack), more than any other issuer, and records its first freeze at 10:21 UTC, more than two hours before Mercado Bitcoin’s public statement._  
  
**By November 9, [the exchange said it had frozen more than 33% of the diverted assets and was replenishing the affected assets with its own funds](https://x.com/MercadoBitcoin/status/2119766691185861022).**

Frozen is not returned. [Bitquery reports that Synthetix removed about $76,000 in SNX from the attacker’s address](https://bitquery.io/investigations/mercado-bitcoin-hack), but does not identify the recipient. The exchange’s updates do not clarify whether that removal is included in its reported figures.

The incident also followed a regulatory milestone. [Mercado Bitcoin filed its application for authorization to provide virtual-asset intermediation and custody on October 27](https://www.letsmoney.com.br/cripto/mercado-bitcoin-pede-licenca-bc-corretora-cripto/), ahead of the [October 30 filing deadline](https://exame.com/future-of-money/mercado-bitcoin-sofre-ataque-hacker-estimado-em-r-2177-milhoes/).  
  
**Those reports do not establish the application’s outcome or include a central-bank response to the incident.**  
  
A practical question remains about deposit addresses. [Bitquery advises customers not to send coins to old Mercado Bitcoin addresses until the exchange says they are safe](https://bitquery.io/investigations/mercado-bitcoin-hack).  
  
[The exchange’s November 9 update says crypto sent to valid MB addresses will be credited when transfers resume](https://x.com/MercadoBitcoin/status/2119766686538641850), but does not specify whether old addresses will be replaced.  
  
[The reopening begins with access to balances and holdings](https://x.com/MercadoBitcoin/status/2119766674723188751), not withdrawals. The exchange [scheduled Pix withdrawals for the following week](https://x.com/MercadoBitcoin/status/2119766689004847262), after account revalidation and only to accounts in customers’ own names.  
  
**Crypto transfers would follow in a later phase, with no date given.**

_On October 27, [Mercado Bitcoin applied for authorization as a virtual-asset brokerage, custody included](https://www.letsmoney.com.br/cripto/mercado-bitcoin-pede-licenca-bc-corretora-cripto/)._  
  
**Six days later, [an attacker was signing transfers out of its customers' deposit addresses](https://bitquery.io/investigations/mercado-bitcoin-hack).**

The main hot-wallet drain took [about 25 minutes](https://bitquery.io/investigations/mercado-bitcoin-hack); the Ethereum deposit sweep ran [about four hours](https://bitquery.io/investigations/mercado-bitcoin-hack), past the [exchange's claim that it had quickly contained the incident](https://x.com/MercadoBitcoin/status/2117239507263132056).

Bitquery puts the theft at [$48.6 million across 11 chains, with $11.8 million frozen by issuers](https://bitquery.io/investigations/mercado-bitcoin-hack), and at its November 4 holdings snapshot, most of the rest still sat in wallets it attributes to the attacker.

**The exchange says it is [already replenishing affected assets with its own funds](https://x.com/MercadoBitcoin/status/2119766691185861022), but [Monday's initial reopening offered balances](https://x.com/MercadoBitcoin/status/2119766674723188751), not withdrawals, with [Pix withdrawals to customers' own accounts scheduled for the following week, after account revalidation](https://x.com/MercadoBitcoin/status/2119766689004847262), and crypto transfers left to later phases.**

[The exchange’s initial announcement described an incident in “one of our systems,”](https://x.com/MercadoBitcoin/status/2117239507263132056) without identifying which one or saying whether the keys behind the affected deposit addresses had been replaced.

[If Bitquery's BitMart attribution holds](https://bitquery.io/investigations/mercado-bitcoin-hack), the same attacker found signing power at two exchanges in a single night, and the investigation identifies neither a common supplier nor an entry point.

**Custody is what Mercado Bitcoin [applied for authorization to provide](https://www.letsmoney.com.br/cripto/mercado-bitcoin-pede-licenca-bc-corretora-cripto/).**
